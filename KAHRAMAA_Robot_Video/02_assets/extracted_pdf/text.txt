ExecutiveSummary
KAHRAMAAcurrentlycleanspotable-waterreservoirsthroughamanualprocessthatrequiresreservoir
shutdown,draining,andhumanentry. Approximately15–20workersmayenterareservoirduringcleaning,
and the process can take between two weeks and one month. Reservoirs vary significantly in size and
configuration,withcapacitiesrangingfromapproximately1to70millionimperialgallonsandwaterdepths
reaching 50 m. These conditions create challenges related to worker safety, water loss, reservoir downtime,
cleaningconsistency, and navigationaround internal structures.
This projectaims to developan intelligent automated reservoir-cleaning systemthatcan remove sediment,
sludge, and debris while the reservoir remains full and in operation, eliminating the need for human
entry during routine cleaning. The system must also protect drinking-water quality, minimize debris
redistribution,navigateautonomouslyaroundinternalobstacles,supportremotemonitoringandmanual
override,andallow safe recovery and practicalservicing.
FollowingDR1, the team refined the project into 12 customer needs, 12 engineering requirements, and 22
measurable engineering metrics. The House of Quality identified turbidity control, autonomous navigation,
andreliablesystemoperationasimportanttechnicalpriorities. Functionalmodelingwasthenusedtodefine
the required material, energy, and information flows. These functions were organized into four system
modules: Power andActuation; Cleaning and DebrisHandling;Mobility, Deployment andRecovery;and
Sensing, Controland Monitoring.
Concepts were generated using brainstorming, 6-3-5 brainwriting, TRIZ contradiction analysis, and a
morphological matrix. Seven complete system concepts were developed and evaluated. A Pugh matrix
first screened the alternatives and identified Concepts C1, C2, and C4 as the strongest candidates. The
subsequentIIAEevaluationrankedC1firstwithaweightedscoreof3556.20,followedbyC2at3357.36
and C4at 3086.44. The differencebetween the twohighestscores exceededthe50-pointtiethreshold.
The selected concept, C1, is a battery-powered tracked crawler using a soft rotating brush within a suction
hood,onboardpumping,hydrocycloneseparation,polishingfiltration,andremovabledebrisstorage. Its
trackedplatformprovidesstablemovementalongthereservoirfloor,whileonboardsensingandcontrol
supportautonomousnavigation, monitoring, andsafe recoverywithoutroutine humanentry.

Thenextstagewillfocusondetailedcomponentselection,subsystemsizing,CADdevelopment,engineering
calculations, interface refinement, and validation planning. A small-scale proof of concept will then be
developed to evaluate the key cleaning, mobility, debris-handling, sensing, and control functions under
representative operatingconditions.

1. Introduction
KAHRAMAA,theQatarGeneralElectricityandWaterCorporation,wasestablishedin2000underLaw
No. 10 and is the owner and operator of Qatar’s electricity and water networks[1]. Qatar’s drinking water
is produced at desalination plants and stored in large reservoirs before distribution to homes and buildings.
KAHRAMAAreportsapproximately2,417millionimperialgallonsofwater-storagecapacityacrossits
reservoirnetwork[2]. Thesereservoirsalsosupportwater-supplyreliabilityduringperiodsofhighdemand
or interruption [3]. Over time, sediment and sludge can accumulate on reservoir surfaces, while corrosion
products and biological growth may also develop. Regular cleaning and disinfection are therefore required
to maintain safe operatingconditions[3,4].
KAHRAMAA currently cleans its reservoirs manually at intervals of approximately five years. Before
cleaning, the reservoir is taken out of service and drained until approximately 20 cm of water remains.
Accordingtotheteam’ssponsordiscussions,around15–20workersenterthereservoirtocleanthefloor
and walls using brushes and high-pressure water jets, while an additional worker remains near the entrance
forsafety. TheWHOseparatelyrecommendskeepinganattendantoutsidethetankaccesshatchduring
entry[4]. Followingcleaning,thereservoirisdisinfected,flushed,refilled,andtestedbeforebeingreturned
to service. The completeprocessmayrequire approximatelytwoweekstoonemonth.
Thisprocesscreatesseveralchallenges. Humanentryexposespersonneltoconfined-spacehazards,slippery
surfaces, chemicals, restricted access, and elevated temperatures [4]. Reservoir shutdown temporarily
reducesavailablestoragecapacity,whiledrainingresultsinthelossoftreatedwaterdespiteKAHRAMAA’s
objectiveofreducingwaterlosses[2]. Cleaningperformancealsodependsonmanualexecution,andthe
existing process does not provide a detailed record of which areas have been cleaned. The design challenge
isfurtherincreasedbythevariationbetweenreservoirs. Reservoirsmaybecircularorrectangular,range
from approximately 1 to 70 million imperial gallons (MIG), reach water depths of up to 50 m, and contain
internal columns,pipes,ladders, inlets,and outlets.
The project originated directly from KAHRAMAA rather than through a market survey or request for
proposals. Theteampreparedteninterviewquestionsanddiscussedthemwiththesponsorduringtheinitial
projectmeetings. SponsormeetingsareexpectedtotakeplaceonMondays,subjecttotheavailabilityof
the sponsor focal point and the representative assigned to the project, and meeting minutes are maintained
to document decisions and feedback. The team also conducted a reservoir site visit on 21 September 2026,
reviewedKAHRAMAA’sdrinking-water-qualityrequirements, andstudiedguidanceforwater-storage-tank
cleaninganddisinfection[4,5]. Animportantclarificationfromthesponsordiscussionswasthateliminating
human entry does not require eliminating all human involvement; an operator may still remotely supervise
the system,intervenewhennecessary, and initiate anemergencystop.
The project therefore aims to develop an intelligent automated system that can clean potable-water
reservoirs while they remain full and in operation, eliminating the need for human entry during routine
cleaning. The system must protect drinking-water quality, collect and remove sediment, sludge, and debris
without unacceptable redistribution, operate with minimal human intervention,avoid damageto reservoir
infrastructure, and provide monitoring of robot position and cleaning progress. The project scope is
limited to the development and evaluation of a small-scale proof of concept under representative controlled
conditions.
During DR1, the team established the initial customer needs, problem definition, engineering requirements,
and House of Quality. Since then, these elements have been refined using additional sponsor feedback,
siteinformation,and designanalysis. The team has also completedfunctional modeling, ideageneration,
concept refinement, concept selection, and preliminary product-architecture development. These activities
provide the basisforthedesigndecisions andfurther developmentpresented in thisreport.
2. BackgroundResearch
The World Health Organization (WHO) describes a conventional process for cleaning and disinfecting
water-storage tanks. The tank is first drained, after which internal surfaces are scrubbed using brushes,
detergent, or high-pressure water jets. The tank is then disinfected using chlorine before being flushed,
refilled, and returned to service [4]. The cleaning equipment, including hoses, pumps, and pipes, must also
be cleaned to preventcontaminants frombeing reintroducedintothetank[4].
5
--- page 7
Each stage of this process performs a different function. Draining provides access to the internal surfaces,
mechanicalcleaningremovesdepositsthatcannotbeeliminatedthroughdisinfectionalone,andchlorination
reduces remaining biological contamination. Therefore, an automated system operating while the reservoir
remains full must still perform the required cleaning functions while avoiding contamination of the stored
water. WHO guidance also identifies cleaning approaches that can reduce the need for direct human entry,
supportingthe project’sobjectiveofeliminatingpersonnel entry during routinecleaning[4].
Sediment accumulation is an important consideration because deposits in drinking-water storage tanks
arenotuniform. Lytleetal. examinedsedimentsamplesfromdrinking-waterstoragetanksandreported
substantial differences in particle size, density, composition, and shape between samples [6]. The deposits
included iron and aluminum compounds, calcium-containing material, organic matter, and sand-like
particles. Fine and lightweight particles can be easily resuspended, which increases the importance of
capturing sediment closetothe cleaning interface rather thanallowing itto disperse intothe reservoir.
Sediment can also reduce the effectiveness of disinfection. Liu et al. investigated chlorine penetration
into drinking-water storage-tank sediment and showed that disinfectant penetration may be limited within
deepersedimentlayers[7]. Thisfurthersupportsphysicallyremovingaccumulateddepositsratherthan
relyingonlyonchemicaldisinfection. Fortheproposedsystem,thecleaningmechanismmusttherefore
mobilize andcollectsedimentwhile limiting redistribution into thesurrounding potablewater.
Workersafetyprovidesanothermajormotivationforautomation. Reservoirsmeetthegeneralcharacteristics
ofconfinedspacesbecausepersonnelcanphysicallyenterthem,butaccessandexitarerestrictedandthe
spacesarenotintendedforcontinuousoccupancy. OSHAidentifieshazardsassociatedwithconfined-space
entry,includinghazardousatmospheresanddifficultaccessconditions[8]. Sponsor-providedoperating
information indicatesthatwatertemperaturesmayreachapproximately 45◦C andoperating water depths
may reach 50 m. At a depth of 50 m, the hydrostatic gauge pressure is approximately 490.5 kPa, or 4.9 bar,
confirming that directhumanoperationin a fullreservoir isnotpractical.
Water quality must also remain protected throughout robotic deployment. KAHRAMAA specifies physical,
chemical, and microbiological requirements for drinking water, including limits or operating ranges for
turbidity,residualchlorine,andmicrobiologicalindicatorssuchasE.coliandtotalcoliforms[5]. These
requirementsmeanthatsuccessfulcleaningcannotbeevaluatedonlybyvisualremovalofsediment. The
system must also prevent unacceptablechangesto theexistingwater conditions duringcleaning.
Material selection is therefore important. NSF/ANSI 61 establishes health-effects requirements for
componentsandmaterialsthatcomeintocontactwithdrinkingwater[9]. Relevantwater-contactcomponents
may include coatings, seals, lubricants, hoses, housings, and other mechanical parts. Consequently,
componentscannotbeselectedonlyonthebasisofstrength,corrosionresistance,ormechanicalperformance;
their suitabilityforpotable-waterservicemustalsobe considered.
Severalexistingroboticsystemsdemonstratethattankandreservoircleaningcanbeperformedwithout
routine human entry. Subsea Tech’s AspiROV is a remotely operated cleaning system designed for
drinking-watertanks. Itusessuctiontocollectsedimentfromthetankfloorthroughahoseconnectedto
external pumpingequipment andcan operate at depths up to 100 m[10]. The published average cleaning
rateofapproximately12.5m2/hprovidesausefulbenchmarkfortheproposedsystem. However,thesystem
remainsexternallyconnectedandoperator controlled,which differsfrom theteam’sgoalofdeveloping a
battery-poweredautonomous platform.
Lee et al. developed the PIRO-U3 underwater cleaning robot for industrial reservoirs [11]. The system
uses tracked locomotion and a submersible pump to remove accumulated material while the reservoir
remains filled. It can follow predefined cleaning paths, demonstrating the feasibility of combining tracked
underwatermovementwithautomatedcoverage. Thestudyalsoreportedthatheavysludgeloadingcan
disturb the robot’s motion and require path correction, showing that cleaning load, traction, and navigation
performance must beconsidered together[11].
WEDA’s VR-50 provides another relevant commercial benchmark. The remote-controlled system is
intended for drinking-water reservoir cleaning and includes a submersible pump, camera, and lighting
system [12]. Its published pump capacity of up to 600 L/min provides an additional comparison for
preliminary suction-system sizing, although the M3 target in Section 5 is based on AspiROV. WEDA also
offerstheVR-600forlargedrinking-waterreservoirs;thisremote-operatedmodelisusedintheupdated
HoQ competitive assessment [13]. Tank-cleaning applications outside the drinking-water industry provide

studies emphasized the importance of debris capture, controlled cleaning, and potable-water-safe materials.
Existing robotic systems provided benchmarks for operating depth, cleaning rate, pumping capability,

ThefundamentalproblemhasnotchangedsinceDR1. KAHRAMAAstillrequiresasaferandmoreeffective

The thirteen morphological-matrix functions provided the structure for assembling each complete concept.

14. UpdatedConclusions
DR2 advanced the project from problem clarification and requirement development to the selection
andpreliminaryarchitectureofacompletereservoir-cleaningconcept. ThefundamentalKAHRAMAA
problemidentified inDR1remains unchanged: reservoircleaningshouldeliminatehumanentry,protect
drinking-water quality, avoid routine shutdown and draining, and provide effective removal of accumulated
sediment, sludge, and debris. The work completed since DR1 has provided a more detailed understanding
ofhowthose objectivescan be translated intoanengineering system.
The customer analysis resulted in twelve finalized needs supported by twelve engineering requirements
and 22 measurable metrics. The updated House of Quality showed that turbidity control, underwater
navigation, and fail-safe operation are among the most influential technical priorities. Functional modeling
then established the material, energy, and information flows required to perform the cleaning process
without prematurely prescribing aphysicalsolution.
Brainstorming, 6-3-5 brainwriting, TRIZ, and morphological analysis produced a broad solution space
thatwasrefinedintosevencompletesystemconcepts. ThePughscreeningidentifiedC1,C2,andC4as
thestrongestcandidates,withC1receivingthehighestnetscoreof+3. ThesubsequentIIAEevaluation
ranked C1 first with a weighted score of 3556.20, compared with 3357.36 for C2 and 3086.44 for C4. The
198.84-point separation between C1 and C2 exceededthe 50-point tie threshold.
Based on these results, the team selected C1 – Battery Tracked Brush–Hydrocyclone Crawler for continued
development. The selected concept combines stable tracked mobility, a soft rotating brush within a
suctionhood,onboardpumping,hydrocycloneseparation,polishingfiltration,removabledebrisstorage,
autonomoussensingandcontrol,remotesupervision,andrecoveryprovisions. Theconceptwasselected
becauseitprovidesthestrongestbalanceofcleaningcapability,water-qualityprotection,navigationstability,
serviceability,anddevelopmentfeasibility among the alternatives considered.
Theselectedconceptwassubsequentlyorganizedintofourmodules: Power&Actuation;Cleaning&Debris
