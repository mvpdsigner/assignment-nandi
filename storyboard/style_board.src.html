Four ways the same video can look and move

While we wait for your answers, here are four visual directions for the video. The story, the 7 sections, the continuous camera and your five-colour palette stay the same in all of them. Only the look and the way things move change. Each option page shows the same three moments (the device, the image transfer and the live readings), so you can compare them side by side. All frames use your real images and values.

AClean Flat

The style of the current storyboard. Light cream world, rounded white cards, soft shadows and smooth eased motion.

Calm · academic · clear

BBlueprint

Engineering drawing on a dark-green grid. Line-art parts, numbered callouts, monospace readouts and schematic wiring for the data.

Technical · precise · engineered

CIsometric World

Every component sits on one 2.5D map, and the camera glides across it following the data. This is the most literal take on "one continuous world".

Playful · spatial · story-like

DBold Editorial

Big type, colour-blocked split screens, and photo "stickers" with tilted tags. Numbers become headlines, and text animates with energy.

Bold · modern · high-energy

What stays the same in every option

  • The 7 sections, timing and continuous camera from Storyboard v3.
  • Your palette: sage, dark green, light sage, cream and charcoal.
  • Your real images and readings. No invented values.
  • No narration or music. 1920 × 1080 MP4.

How to choose

  • Reply with a letter (A, B, C or D).
  • Or mix them, e.g. "C, with B's callout labels".
  • The last page compares the options and gives our recommendation.

Isometric World

One map, the camera travels across itPlayful · spatial · story-like
Devicesection 1
Image transfersection 4
Live readingssection 6

Look

  • One isometric board holds every station: device, website, tower, ngrok, Flask, desktop and Blynk.
  • Simple 3-tone blocks in the palette; your real images float above as cards.

Motion

  • A single camera flies over the map from station to station, so there are no cuts at all.
  • The photo hops along arcs with a shadow on the ground; data travels as small cubes.
  • At each stop the camera dives into a flat close-up (the website, the Python window), then rises back to the map.

Type & data objects

  • Inter; flat, front-facing cards so text never gets skewed.
ngrok
Strengths: answers your request most directly. The audience sees the whole system as one place and watches the data travel through it.
Watch-out: the most work to build. Your device appears as an illustrated model on the map, with the real render shown in the close-ups.

Our recommendation: C + B, an isometric map with blueprint detail. The camera flies over one isometric map (C), which most directly answers "one continuous story where the camera follows the data". When it reaches a station, it dives into a close-up drawn with B's numbered callouts, wiring and monospace readouts, so the technical detail stays clear for a design review. Your real images (device, website, rooftop photo and shading stages) are always shown in full colour in those close-ups.

If you prefer something simpler: B on its own gives a strong engineering look with less build work. If you like the current storyboard frames: A stays as it is, and we add more camera movement.
